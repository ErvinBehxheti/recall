Photosynthesis

Biology, Year 9, Unit 4: Plants

Learning objectives

  • Describe what photosynthesis is and why plants need it
  • Name the inputs and products of photosynthesis
  • Explain the two stages: light reactions and the Calvin cycle
  • Explain what limits the rate of photosynthesis
2

What is photosynthesis?

Plants are producers: they make their own food.

  • They use energy from sunlight
  • They turn water and carbon dioxide into glucose (a sugar)
  • Oxygen is released as a by-product
3

The word equation

carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

Needs: light energy and chlorophyll

4

The symbol equation

6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2

Six molecules of carbon dioxide and six of water make one glucose and six oxygen.

5

What do plants take in?

  • Water: absorbed by the roots, carried up in the xylem
  • Carbon dioxide: enters the leaf through tiny holes called stomata
  • Light: captured by the leaves
6

Inside a leaf

Upper epidermis
Palisade layer (most chloroplasts)
Spongy layer (air spaces)
Lower epidermis with stomata

Leaves are thin and flat to catch light and let gases move in and out.

7

Stomata and guard cells

  • Stomata are tiny pores, mostly on the underside of the leaf
  • Guard cells open and close them
  • Open: carbon dioxide in, oxygen and water vapour out
8

Chloroplasts

  • Photosynthesis happens inside chloroplasts
  • Found in leaf cells, especially the palisade layer
  • Contain stacks of membranes called thylakoids
  • The fluid around them is called the stroma
9

Chlorophyll

  • The green pigment inside chloroplasts
  • Absorbs red and blue light best
  • Reflects green light, so leaves look green

Blue: absorbed    Green: reflected    Red: absorbed

10

Two stages

1. Light reactions

Need light. Happen in the thylakoids.

2. Calvin cycle

Do not need light directly. Happen in the stroma.

11

Stage 1: light-dependent reactions

  • Light energy splits water molecules
  • Oxygen is released (this is the oxygen we breathe)
  • Energy is stored in carrier molecules ATP and NADPH
12

Stage 2: the Calvin cycle

  • Also called the light-independent reactions
  • Uses ATP and NADPH from stage 1
  • Carbon dioxide is fixed into glucose
13

What do plants do with glucose?

  • Respiration: release energy for the plant
  • Make cellulose to build cell walls
  • Store it as starch for later
14

Why does it matter?

  • Almost every food chain starts with a plant
  • Puts oxygen into the air
  • Removes carbon dioxide from the air
15

Limiting factors

  • Light intensity
  • Carbon dioxide concentration
  • Temperature (enzymes)

When one factor goes up, the rate rises until another factor runs short.

16

Summary

  • Plants use light to make glucose from carbon dioxide and water
  • Chlorophyll in chloroplasts captures the light
  • Light reactions release oxygen; the Calvin cycle builds glucose
  • Light, carbon dioxide and temperature control the rate
