The Causes of World War I

History, Year 9, Unit 2: The Great War

Learning objectives

  • Identify the long-term causes of World War I
  • Explain how the assassination in Sarajevo started the war
  • Judge which cause was most important
2

Europe in 1914

  • The great powers: Britain, France, Germany, Russia, Austria-Hungary
  • Competing for land, trade and influence
  • Long-term tensions had been building for decades
3

Remember MAIN

Strong pride in your nation

4

Militarism

  • Armies grew quickly in the years before 1914
  • Naval race: Britain and Germany built huge battleships called dreadnoughts
  • Leaders trusted military power to solve problems
5

War plans

  • Countries prepared detailed plans for war
  • Germany's Schlieffen Plan: defeat France quickly, then turn to Russia
  • The plan meant marching through neutral Belgium
6

Alliances

Triple Alliance

Germany
Austria-Hungary
Italy

Triple Entente

France
Russia
Britain

A war between two countries could pull in all their partners.

7

Imperialism

  • Empires meant wealth, raw materials and status
  • Britain and France had the largest empires
  • Germany wanted colonies too, causing rivalry in Africa and Asia
8

Nationalism

  • Strong pride in your nation, sometimes believing it is better than others
  • Made many people eager to support a war
  • Slavic groups like the Serbs wanted freedom from Austria-Hungary
9

The Balkans: "the powder keg of Europe"

  • Serbia wanted to unite Slavic peoples
  • Austria-Hungary saw this as a threat to its empire
  • Russia supported Serbia
10

Sarajevo, 28 June 1914

  • Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to Austria-Hungary, visits Sarajevo
  • He is shot by Gavrilo Princip, a young Bosnian Serb
  • Princip was helped by a secret Serbian nationalist group, the Black Hand
  • Austria-Hungary blames Serbia
11

From one shot to a world war

  • 28 July Austria-Hungary declares war on Serbia
  • 30 July Russia mobilizes to protect Serbia
  • 1 August Germany declares war on Russia
  • 3 August Germany declares war on France
  • 4 August Germany invades Belgium; Britain declares war
12

Summary

Long-term causes

Militarism, alliances, imperialism, nationalism

Short-term cause

The assassination of Franz Ferdinand

13

Discussion

Which cause was the most important? Use evidence to explain your answer.
